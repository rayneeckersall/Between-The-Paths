Project Overview

An Allegory of the Cave in Augmented Reality

Between The Paths is an AR interactive narrative built by Mythos Studio — Rayne, Rianna, and Livia — for VDES34444 Interactive Narrative at Sheridan College. The experience is rooted in Plato's Allegory of the Cave: a philosophical meditation on perception, habit, and how awareness reshapes the way we interpret reality.

Users explore a mysterious augmented forest overlaid on Sheridan's Trafalgar campus. Through a physical poster, a landing page, an AR scavenger hunt, and two branching Instagram epilogues, the experience asks one question: when you see two paths, which do you trust?

"Our narrative emphasizes personal interpretation rather than resolution or conclusion — perception, awareness, and choice are strengthened through interaction."

Narrative Development

The Story & Its Arcs

Our story places the user in a looping forest path with no initial context or objective, encouraging passive progression and familiarity. Subtle disruptions gradually invite the user to question the nature of their environment. A mysterious owl reveals hidden truths. Map fragments must be collected. Then — a choice.

Crucially, the experience subverts the user's expectations. The path that looks safe is the trap. The path that looks dangerous is the way out. This bait-and-switch is the allegory's core: we follow what appears trustworthy without questioning it.

Arc One

Comfort & Conformity

The user finds themselves on a forest path with no explanation, goal, or sense of purpose. They move forward alongside NPCs, following the path without questioning it. The experience establishes routine and comfort, while quietly limiting the user's agency.

Arc Two

Disruption & Discovery

The user senses the experience is unnatural. A mysterious creature — the Owl — appears, acknowledging the user and revealing the unsettling nature of the world. Hidden map fragments become visible. The user must collect all four to gain understanding and break out of the loop.

Arc Three

The Choice

With the full map revealed, the illusion is broken — but certainty is still unclear. Two paths appear: one familiar and beautiful, the other unknown and unmarked. The user chooses whether to remain in comfort or step into uncertainty, with no guaranteed outcome.

Characters

The User

The player-character. They explore the forest and navigate the experience — the primary vessel for the allegory.

The NPCs

Silent figures populating the forest, walking ahead of the user. They represent individuals who follow conformity without question.

The Owl

A mysterious creature that lives in the forest. Originally a guide, the owl was revised to be a manipulator — it works for the forest, steering users toward the "bad" path presented as safety.

Themes

Comfort vs. Truth

Is it better to live pleasantly in illusion or painfully in reality?

Perception & Illusion

What we accept as "normal" is rarely unexamined.

Choice & Free Will

Agency only matters when the options are genuinely understood.

Awareness & Conformity

The Construct's power is sustained by obedience — awareness is the first step toward breaking it.

Experience Structure

The experience unfolds across five distinct phases, each on a different platform:

Discovery
Physical Poster → Landing Page

User scans a QR code on a physical poster placed in campus. This takes them to the landing page — a dark forest environment coded in HTML/CSS with Claude's assistance.

Introduction
AR Experience Loads

User enters the AR experience from the landing page. The Sheridan campus is overlaid with stylized forest trees, NPCs, and the owl character anchored via MultiSet VPS scanning.

Scene 2
Owl Encounter & Map Fragments

User meets the owl, which introduces the concept of map fragments. Wayfinding guides them to each of four locations: SCAET, the Library, the Learning Commons, and B Wing.

Scene 3
The Final Map

Collecting all four fragments reveals the complete map. A custom-scripted overlay appears showing two diverging paths — one inviting, one ominous.

Scene 4
Instagram Epilogues

The user is redirected to one of two Instagram accounts depending on their choice, each with five posts unfolding the narrative's true meaning.

The Bait & Switch

The original concept positioned the owl as a reliable, helpful guide. During production, the team revised this substantially. The owl now misleads the user into choosing the "bad" path — which is presented as the beautiful, safe option. The owl works for the forest and wants to keep as many people trapped there as possible.

Correspondingly, The Deep Unknown Instagram (visually dark and ominous) is actually the path to truth and freedom, while The Forest Path Instagram (warm, inviting) reveals that the user is still trapped. This inversion is the experience's central philosophical statement.

AR / VR Development

Building the Augmented Forest

The team conducted extensive platform research before committing to Zappar/Mattercraft. Three platforms were evaluated: Unity, Snap Lens Studio, and Zappar. Unity's complexity and Lens Studio's outdated tutorials both ruled them out. Zappar's VPS tracking and no-download browser experience made it the best fit.

VPS Scanning: From Immersal to MultiSet

Zappar supports two VPS providers. Immersal was tested first — scans came out pixelated and objects anchored below floor level. MultiSet proved far superior: Rayne successfully scanned the entire campus route in one session, and objects stayed reliably anchored during mobile testing. Livia's device was incompatible with MultiSet's scanning app, so the scan was Rayne's primary responsibility.

Key Technical Implementations (Rayne)

Map Fragment Counter

A custom TypeScript script — developed with Claude — makes each map fragment disappear on tap and increments a counter. When all four are collected, the final map overlay appears.

Wayfinding System

Wayfinding updates one location at a time rather than showing the full route. When a fragment or artifact is scanned, the waypoint updates. Keeps the experience accessible regardless of campus familiarity.

Custom States & Timelines

Behaviors, active states, and timeline-driven animations were built in Mattercraft to control when assets appear and how interactions trigger events.

Image Tracking

Livia implemented image tracking in Zappar, training the system to recognize physical artifacts. When the camera sees an artifact, text and animation appear — bridging the physical and digital layers.

Mobile UI Optimization

During development, Rayne noticed the counter and dialog UI were clipping at mobile resolutions. Multiple rounds of testing across screen sizes were conducted to resize elements until they behaved correctly. This iterative, device-first approach ensured the experience worked for users directly on campus.

Digital Media Development

Landing Page & Instagram Epilogues

Landing Page — Developed with Claude

The landing page was built iteratively with Claude as a co-developer. The team sketched their vision, shared it with Claude, and refined the output across 12+ iterations. Early versions were too plain; later iterations refined the forest tree aesthetics, solved mobile display issues with tree trunk rendering, and nailed the brown-toned colour palette that implies depth and distance.

The final page is hosted on GitHub and uses pure HTML/CSS to render a moody, atmospheric forest environment. A single "Enter The Path" button loads the AR experience. In Project 3, Claude was again used to add the live AR link to this button.

Live: mercerri.github.io/betweenthepaths/

Instagram Accounts (Rianna)

Both Instagram accounts function as the experience's ending — the user lands on one depending on their path choice. Each features five posts following a classic narrative arc: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and denouement. The forest path posts use warm, welcoming imagery; the deep unknown posts use darkness and shadow — but their underlying meanings are inverted.

@the.forest_path

The "bad" ending presented beautifully. Five posts reveal the owl was manipulating the user and that they remain trapped in the forest — they chose what felt safe.

@the.deep_unknown

The "good" ending presented ominously. Five posts reveal this path never hid itself — it waited. The user didn't choose wrong; they chose freedom.

Asset Development

Visual Language & Physical Artifacts

All visual assets use a stylized 2D vector illustration style — warm-toned, storybook-inspired, with soft textures and glowing elements. This approach was chosen deliberately: photorealism would have required render infrastructure beyond scope, and the stylized look is more cohesive across the AR and Instagram layers.

Digital Assets

Trees

Multiple tree variants — some with foliage, some without — generated using Adobe Illustrator's Text to Vector Graphic tool. Variety prevents the forest from feeling repetitive and flat.

NPCs

Figures with backs facing the camera, appearing to walk in front of the user. Their anonymity reinforces the conformity theme — faceless, directionless followers.

Map Fragments

Four collectible fragments that assemble into the final map. Traditional cartographic symbols (trees, mountains, X-marks) make the map feel tactile and earned.

Final Map

The assembled map shows two diverging paths: one marked with skulls and warnings (the "bad" path that looks dangerous but leads to truth), and one with flowers and sparkles (the "good" path that leads to continued entrapment).

Owl

The central character. Stands on the ground and interacts with the user in Scene 2. Visually friendly — which is the point.

Rocks

Terrain variation assets in the same stylized vector style, adding believability to the AR forest floor.

Physical Artifacts (Livia)

Three physical artifacts were placed around campus, each designed to blend into the environment while carrying the consistent Dasos owl logo for recognition. Image tracking lets Zappar detect them and trigger AR overlays.

Artifact 1 — SCAET

A brochure mimicking Sheridan's official viewbooks, acting as a hidden artifact. The owl logo marks it for users who know what to look for.

Artifact 2 — Library

A soil planter marker labelled "Dasos" (Greek for "forest"), resembling standard plant markers placed around campus.

Artifact 3 — Learning Commons

A poster styled to match Sheridan's advertisement displays, using a QR code and art that matches the experience's overall visual language.

Artifact Logo (Livia)

Livia designed the owl logo to represent the owl working for the forest — almost like a surveillance camera, watching the user. Feathers and leaves show the connection between the owl and the forest entities. The logo was hand-drawn, digitized with Gemini AI, then refined in Adobe Illustrator.

Artificial Intelligence

AI was woven throughout every layer of the project — from initial ideation and writing assistance to code generation and asset creation. The team used AI tools purposefully and transparently, documenting each use case in their citations.

Mattercraft AI
  • Image tracking troubleshooting
  • Behavior debugging support
Lens Studio AI

What's notable about the team's AI usage is how purposeful it was. Each tool was chosen for its strengths: Claude for coding and scripting (cited as better than ChatGPT for code), ChatGPT for ideation and writing, Adobe Illustrator AI for rapid visual asset iteration. AI accelerated production without replacing the team's creative direction — the concepts, narrative decisions, and quality judgment remained human.

Process Reflection

What We Learned

This project was the most technically ambitious work the team had undertaken. Building a location-based AR experience from scratch — with no prior ZapWorks/Mattercraft experience, across an unfamiliar platform that was actively changing and deprecating tutorials — required constant adaptation.

On Platform Research

Testing Unity, Lens Studio, and two VPS providers before committing to MultiSet cost real time, but paid off. The team avoided building on a platform that would have bottlenecked the experience — Immersal's messy scans and floor-clipping objects would have undermined the immersive quality entirely.

On Iteration

The landing page's twelve-plus Claude iterations exemplify the team's iterative approach. Rather than treating the first acceptable result as done, they kept refining — addressing specific mobile formatting issues, refining the colour temperature of the tree trunks, improving depth cues. This discipline carried into the AR layer: UI elements were tested across multiple phone sizes before the experience was finalized.

On Narrative Risk

The decision to invert the owl from guide to manipulator was a significant narrative risk taken late in production. It complicated the Instagram content strategy and required revisiting character documentation — but the result is a much more philosophically coherent experience. The bait-and-switch is now the project's strongest idea.

On AI Integration

The team's use of AI tools demonstrates both the power and the limits of AI-assisted creative work. Claude helped unblock coding challenges that would have taken days to solve manually. Adobe Illustrator's AI enabled rapid asset prototyping across different visual themes. But the team's own creative judgment — choosing the stylized vector style, revising the owl's role, writing five emotionally distinct Instagram posts per path — was irreplaceable. AI accelerated execution; the team directed everything meaningful.

On Risk Management

The QR code incident — where a paid QR service was unwittingly used, rendering the code inactive — was caught in time and resolved by generating a new QR code with Claude. This kind of small disaster during production is exactly what risk assessment prepares teams to handle without panic. The team recovered quickly and without losing significant momentum.

Learning Outcomes Addressed

Comprehensive interactive narrative proposal (LO 1), content for varied contexts (LO 6), unique perspectives and innovation (LO 7), personal practice situating (LO 8), group contribution evaluation (LO 9).

Tools Mastered

Zappar/Mattercraft, MultiSet VPS, TypeScript behavior scripting, Adobe Illustrator AI, GitHub Pages hosting, Instagram as narrative platform.

What We'd Do Differently

Begin VPS scanning earlier to allow more iteration time. Test physical artifacts in situ sooner. Document process screenshots more consistently from Week 1 for richer case study assets.
