Project Overview

An Allegory of the Cave in Augmented Reality

Between Two Paths is an AR interactive narrative built by Mythos Studio — Rayne, Rianna, and Livia — for VDES34444 Interactive Narrative at Sheridan College. The experience is rooted in Plato's Allegory of the Cave: a philosophical meditation on perception, habit, and how awareness reshapes the way we interpret reality.

Users explore a mysterious augmented forest overlaid on Sheridan's Trafalgar campus. Through a physical poster, a landing page, an AR scavenger hunt, and two branching Instagram epilogues, the experience asks one question: when you see two paths, which do you trust?

Narrative Development

The Story & Its Arcs

Our story places the user in a looping forest path with no initial context or objective, encouraging passive progression and familiarity. Subtle disruptions gradually invite the user to question the nature of their environment. A mysterious owl reveals hidden truths. Map fragments must be collected. Then — a choice.

Crucially, the experience subverts the user's expectations. The path that looks safe is the trap. The path that looks dangerous is the way out.

Arc One

Comfort & Conformity

The user finds themselves on a forest path with no explanation, goal, or sense of purpose. They move forward alongside NPCs, following the path without questioning it. The experience establishes routine and comfort, while quietly limiting the user's agency.

Arc Two

Disruption & Discovery

The user senses the experience is unnatural. A mysterious creature — the Owl — appears, acknowledging the user and revealing the unsettling nature of the world. Hidden map fragments become visible. The user must collect all four to gain understanding and break out of the loop.

Arc Three

The Choice

With the full map revealed, the illusion is broken — but certainty is still unclear. Two paths appear: one familiar and beautiful, the other unknown and unmarked. The user chooses whether to remain in comfort or step into uncertainty, with no guaranteed outcome.

The Bait & Switch

The original concept positioned the owl as a reliable, helpful guide. During production, the team revised this substantially. The owl now misleads the user into choosing the "bad" path — presented as the beautiful, safe option. This inversion is the experience's central philosophical statement.

AR / VR Development

Building the Augmented Forest

The team evaluated three platforms — Unity, Snap Lens Studio, and Zappar — before committing. Unity's complexity and Lens Studio's outdated tutorials both ruled them out. Zappar's VPS tracking and no-download browser experience made it the best fit.

Key Technical Implementations

Map Fragment Counter

Custom TypeScript script — developed with Claude — disappears each fragment on tap and increments a counter. All four collected triggers the final map overlay.

Wayfinding System

Updates one location at a time. When a fragment or artifact is scanned, the waypoint updates — keeping it accessible to all campus visitors.

States & Timelines

Behaviors, active states, and timeline-driven animations built in Mattercraft to control when assets appear and how interactions trigger events.

Image Tracking

Livia implemented image tracking — the system recognizes physical artifacts and triggers AR text and animation overlays.

Process Reflection

What We Learned

This project was the most technically ambitious work the team had undertaken. Building a location-based AR experience from scratch — with no prior ZapWorks/Mattercraft experience, across an unfamiliar platform actively deprecating its own tutorials — required constant adaptation.

On Platform Research

Testing Unity, Lens Studio, and two VPS providers before committing to MultiSet cost real time, but paid off. Immersal's messy scans and floor-clipping objects would have undermined the experience's immersive quality entirely.

On Iteration

The landing page's twelve-plus Claude iterations exemplify the team's iterative approach — addressing specific mobile formatting issues, refining the colour temperature of tree trunks, improving depth cues. This discipline carried into the AR layer: UI elements were tested across multiple phone sizes before the experience was finalized.

On Narrative Risk

The decision to invert the owl from guide to manipulator was a significant risk taken late in production. It complicated the Instagram content strategy — but the result is a much more philosophically coherent experience. The bait-and-switch is now the project's strongest idea.

On AI Integration

AI accelerated execution — Claude unblocked coding challenges in minutes; Illustrator AI enabled rapid visual prototyping. But the team's creative judgment — the stylized vector style, the owl's revised role, five emotionally distinct posts per path — was irreplaceable. The team directed everything meaningful.

On Risk Management

The QR code incident — a paid service unwittingly used, rendering the code inactive — was resolved quickly by generating a replacement with Claude. Small disasters during production are exactly what risk assessment prepares teams to handle without losing momentum.
